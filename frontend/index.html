Student Depression Prediction

An educational machine-learning project that reads patterns in student survey answers and estimates a lower or higher risk of depression. A way to explore what data can, and can't, tell us about well-being.

Get Started Explore the Project

Twelve short questions, about three minutes. The result is an estimate, never a diagnosis.

What this project does

Students answer a short survey about their studies, their daily habits and a few personal questions. A machine-learning model, trained on a large set of earlier survey responses, looks for patterns in those answers and returns an estimate: a lower or higher estimated risk, together with a probability.

Think of it as a window into how machine learning reads patterns in data, not as a way to judge anyone.

  • Suicidal-thought response

It isn't a diagnosis

No single answer decides the result, and none of these factors is claimed to cause depression. The model only compares how the answers fit together with patterns in earlier data. It can't see your situation, and it is no substitute for talking with a professional.

How it works

From your answers to an estimate, in four steps.

  1. 01

    Answer the survey

    You share information about your academic life, your daily habits and a few personal questions.

  2. 02

    Your answers are prepared

    The application arranges your answers the same way the model saw its training examples, so the two can be compared fairly.

  3. 03

    The model looks for patterns

    A trained machine-learning model compares your answers with patterns it learned from earlier survey data.

  4. 04

    You get an estimate

    You see an estimated result and a probability. It's a starting point for reflection, not a verdict.
